Pitch Trainer (working title)

An app that plays a note, listens to you sing it back, and tells you whether you were too high, too low, or nailed it, and by how much.

The unique kernel

It's the ear you don't have yet. You find out right away, and visually, which way you're off and how far, without waiting for someone in choir to tell you.

How it fits together

Who it's for

Singers who struggle to stay on pitch, people like you. Beginners and experts can use it too, but this version speaks plain language (no "cents" or theory jargon).

"when i sing off, i cant tell until someone tells me and i wont know wheter im too low or too high and its usually when im singing in the choir"

What "working" looks like — the 1-minute demo

App plays a note
You sing it
Dot floats above or below the line, with a "how close" message
You adjust your voice
Dot hits the line: "you nailed it, well done"

The "oh, that's cool" beat: the dot visibly tracking your voice up and down in real time.

The boundary: now, later, cut

Now: the proof of concept 4
  • Play a note, listen through the microphone, detect the sung pitch
  • Floating dot vs. target line + "how close" message (too high / too low / nailed it)
  • A short round of ~5 notes with an end-of-round score
  • Light / dark theme toggle
Stretch: only after the core works 1
  • One tiny ear-training exercise: "I play two notes, which one is higher?"
Later: you want all of these after the PoC 5
  • Ear training for intervals, chords, scales, and chord progressions
  • Timed / untimed note-reading quizzes (treble, bass, alto clefs; instrument fingerboards)
  • Stats dashboard: accuracy history, levels, practice time, streaks
  • Pitch detection for instruments ("listen to your playing")
  • Beginner and expert modes (precise feedback in cents for experts)
Explicitly cut 2
  • Mental health companion: set aside as a separate project; that domain needs safety planning beyond a 2–4 hour PoC.
  • Saving progress across days: moved to Later. A per-round score proves the loop without needing storage or accounts.

Look & feel (starting point)

Light and dark themes. The pitch picture is the star: one big target line and a floating dot that turns green on a hit, with small, calm buttons and scores around it. The tone is encouraging. Details get shaped in 3-prd.

Why it matters to you

You've sung for years but feel "tone deaf". You're building this "for me and for others to use as well," with the bigger goal of giving users "at least basic music training."
